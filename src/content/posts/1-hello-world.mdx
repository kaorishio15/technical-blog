Welcome to my first blog post! I've recently started learning more about cybersecurity, and I decided to create this blog to document what I'm learning, the projects I'm building, and the things I find interesting.

## What is cybersecurity?

Before getting too far into learning cybersecurity, I wanted to understand what cybersecurity actually means.

At its simplest, cybersecurity is about protecting **people, systems, networks, and data** from being accessed, damaged, or misused by people who shouldn't have access to them.

The more I learn about it, the more I realize that cybersecurity isn't just one specific topic. It covers a huge range of areas, from protecting someone's personal account to securing the infrastructure that large organizations rely on.

Some areas that fall under cybersecurity include:

- **Network security** — protecting networks and the systems connected to them
- **Application security** — finding and fixing vulnerabilities in software
- **Cloud security** — protecting applications, data, and infrastructure hosted in the cloud
- **Identity and access management** — controlling who can access what
- **Information security** — protecting sensitive information from unauthorized access or changes
- **Endpoint security** — protecting devices such as laptops, phones, and servers
- **Incident response** — figuring out what happened when a security incident occurs and responding to it
- **Offensive security** — looking at systems from an attacker's perspective to find weaknesses before someone else does

I think one of the most interesting parts of cybersecurity is that all of these areas are connected. A vulnerability in an application could eventually lead to someone gaining access to a system, which could then expose sensitive information.

It's not just about stopping attacks. It's also about understanding **how systems work, where they can go wrong, and how they can be made more secure.**

## Why does cybersecurity matter?

Almost everything we do now involves technology in some way.

We use computers to store personal information, phones to communicate, websites to make purchases, and online services to store everything from photos to important documents. Businesses and organizations rely on technology even more heavily.

That means there is a lot of valuable information and infrastructure that needs to be protected.

Cyberattacks can take many different forms. Someone might try to steal login credentials through phishing, infect a computer with malware, overwhelm a website with traffic, or exploit a vulnerability in an application.

Some common types of cyberattacks and threats include:

- **Phishing**
- **Malware**
- **Ransomware**
- **Credential theft**
- **Denial-of-service attacks**
- **Social engineering**
- **Software vulnerabilities**
- **Attacks against cloud systems**

One thing I've noticed while starting to learn about cybersecurity is that the human side of security is just as important as the technical side.

A system can have strong security controls, but if someone gives away their password, clicks a malicious link, or accidentally exposes sensitive information, an attacker may still find a way in.

## There is a lot to learn

One of the first things I've realized is just how broad cybersecurity is.

I started out thinking of cybersecurity as one subject, but it quickly became clear that there are many different paths within it.

There is networking, cryptography, operating systems, web security, digital forensics, penetration testing, security engineering, cloud security, malware analysis, and much more.

And then there are all of the technologies that cybersecurity professionals have to keep up with.

Cloud computing continues to change how systems are built. Artificial intelligence is creating new tools as well as new security concerns. The number of connected devices continues to grow. New vulnerabilities are discovered all the time.

It's definitely a lot to take in.

But that's also what makes learning cybersecurity interesting to me. There are always new things to understand, and learning one concept often leads to another question.

## What I'm hoping to learn

Since I'm still at the beginning of my cybersecurity journey, I don't want this blog to pretend that I already know everything.

Instead, I want to use it to keep track of what I'm learning along the way.

I'll be writing about topics that I come across while studying, interesting cybersecurity news, projects that I work on, and concepts that I find worth understanding better.

Some of the things I want to explore include:

- Networking and how devices communicate
- Cryptography and encryption
- Authentication and authorization
- Web application security
- Common vulnerabilities and how they work
- Linux and command-line tools
- Security tools and frameworks
- Cloud security
- Cybersecurity projects and labs
- Current cybersecurity news and trends

Hopefully, looking back at these posts later will let me see how much I've learned and how my understanding of cybersecurity has changed over time.

For now, this is just the beginning. Welcome to my cybersecurity journey.
